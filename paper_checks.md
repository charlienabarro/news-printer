# Paper checks

Facts checked against the full text, so they don't need re-checking.

## van de Werken et al. (2010), J Sleep Res 19(3):425–435

"Effects of artificial dawn on sleep inertia, skin temperature, and the awakening cortisol
response". doi:10.1111/j.1365-2869.2010.00828.x. Full text read on 16 Nov 2026 from the
University of Groningen repository (publisher's PDF).

- **Participants:** 16 healthy people who report difficulty waking up. Control night versus
  artificial-dawn night, in random order.
- **Light:** Philips Wake-up Light with a 105 W halogen bulb, two lights either side of the bed.
  Light rose over the last 30 min before the alarm to a maximum of 300 lux (measured at eye
  level, 40 cm away) and stayed on after the alarm. Control: the light switched on at 300 lux at
  the alarm.
- **Shape of the rise (Fig. 5 and text):** low for the first 10 min; 5.4 to 17.8 lux between
  minutes 10 and 15; then up to 300 lux by the alarm.
- **Results:** with artificial dawn, people felt less sleepy and more active over the 90 min
  after waking. No significant effect on task performance, core body temperature or cortisol.
  The authors link the benefit to more light sleep or wakefulness in the last 30 min and a
  faster drop in skin temperature after getting up.
- **Caveats:** small sample (16); funded partly by Philips; the study did not compare light
  colours, so it says nothing about red or orange versus white.
